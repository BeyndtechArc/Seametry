A bundle assembles tokens. An ETF adds a share whose creation and redemption
loop remains open to participants on both sides of the market. The basket is a
mechanism. This page takes that apart one question at a time, and every
instrument on it runs the same arithmetic and reads the same evidence as the
product.

## What do I actually hold when I buy a tokenized stock?

A claim, not the share itself. The token in your wallet is a promise from
whoever issued it, and different issuers make different promises under similar
symbols.

Think of a cloakroom ticket. The ticket is not the coat. What it gets you back,
and from whom, depends on what is written on the ticket. A tokenized stock is
the ticket; the Grade says what is written on it.

The Grade describes the legal shape of the claim. It is never a score of
quality, and Seametry does not turn it into one.

## What can the issuer still do to my token?

More than most people expect. A token built on Solana's Token-2022 standard can
carry powers its issuer keeps after you buy it: freezing it where it sits,
pausing every transfer, taking it back from any wallet, changing how many you
appear to hold. These powers are written into the token's mint account, so they
can be read directly instead of taken on trust.

## Why is a bundle of tokens not an ETF?

Because a bundle stops when the tokens arrive. You hold several tokens, and
that is all. There is no separate share, no fixed recipe behind that share, and
no way for anyone else to close a gap between the price of the parts and the
price of the whole.

An ETF has a loop. Participants can turn the underlying assets into fund shares
(creation) and fund shares back into the underlying assets (redemption). That
loop is what keeps a share honest to its contents.

<LoopDiagram />

The Hall is that loop, on chain. A Strike deposits the recipe, which Seametry
calls the Formula, and issues shares. A Melt destroys shares and records a
Claim on every constituent.

## How does a Strike turn constituents into shares?

You choose how many shares you want, and the Hall works out exactly how much of
each constituent that costs. You never choose an amount of money; you choose a
number of shares. That rule exists so nobody can be rounded down to zero shares
by a clever deposit, which is how a well-known kind of attack on shared vaults
works.

For each constituent the Hall computes:

```text
pay   = ceil( shares × held_by_the_hall ÷ shares_in_existence )
```

and when shares are melted:

```text
claim = floor( shares × held_by_the_hall ÷ shares_in_existence )
```

Paying rounds up. Receiving rounds down. Every rounding favours the Hall, so the
people already holding shares can never be diluted by someone shaving
fractions. Move the slider and watch what that means:

<StrikeMelt />

At the starting state, one share costs one atom to Strike, and Melting it
straight back credits zero. That is not a fee; it is the fraction that rounding
keeps in the Hall, so a round trip can never be used to take value for free.

## What happens when I Melt, and what if an issuer freezes one constituent?

This is the question the Hall was built to answer. The obvious way to redeem a
basket is to burn the shares and send every constituent back in one
transaction. If any single issuer has frozen its token, that one transfer fails,
and because it is one transaction, everything fails with it. One issuer's power
over one stock becomes a veto over your whole exit.

The Hall splits the exit in two. Melt burns your shares and writes down what
you are owed of every constituent; it touches no constituent at all, so no
freeze, pause or hook can reach it. Then each constituent is withdrawn on its
own. A frozen one stays yours as a named Claim, and the others arrive.

The same split shows in the program's own instructions:

```text
redeem    burns shares, credits a Claim on every leg     touches no constituent mint
withdraw  delivers one leg of a Claim                    touches that one mint
create    pulls exactly the required units, mints shares touches every mint
```

## Who is allowed to Strike and Melt?

Anyone. There is no approval list. Any wallet holding the required constituents
can Strike shares, and any wallet holding shares can Melt them, without asking
Seametry or anyone else.

In a traditional ETF only approved institutions, called authorized
participants, may create and redeem. On the Hall the authorized participant set
is everyone.

<ClaimLine lead="Delivery stays explicit.">A constituent held back by its issuer remains a named Claim and can be withdrawn when delivery resumes.</ClaimLine>

## Does the Hall ever need a price?

No. Strike and Melt move quantities, not values. Neither instruction reads an
oracle, a quote or a net asset value, so no stale or manipulated price can
change what a Strike costs or what a Melt returns.

```text
Strike 10 shares   pays exact constituent quantities   price never read
Melt 10 shares     credits exact constituent claims    price never read
```

Prices still matter to you as a buyer; they just never instruct the Hall.
