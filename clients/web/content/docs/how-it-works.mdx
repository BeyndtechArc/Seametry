---
title: How it works
description: Why a tokenized-stock bundle is not an ETF, and what the Hall changes.
---

A bundle assembles tokens. An ETF adds a share whose creation and redemption
loop remains open to participants on both sides of the market. The basket is a
mechanism.

## A claim is not the underlying.

What does a tokenized stock put in the holder's wallet?

A token can represent a security entitlement, a certificate that settles to
cash, an interest in a vehicle holding private shares, or a claim the Office
has not classified.

Those legal shapes can trade under similar symbols while giving the holder
different routes back to the thing named on screen.

<Stamp kind="warn" reason="Grade describes legal shape, never quality." />

The issuer's on-chain controls travel beside the Grade. Seametry does not turn
either one into a score.

## A bundle stops at assembly.

What is missing after several tokens arrive in one transaction?

The holder receives several constituent tokens. There is no separate share, no
fixed Formula behind that share, and no mechanism that lets another participant
close a gap between the parts and the whole.

The loop begins at the Hall. A Strike deposits the Formula and issues shares. A
Melt destroys shares and records a claim on every constituent. Delivery follows
each issuer's current controls without undoing the claim.

## The authorized participant set is everyone.

Who can create shares or return them to the Formula?

No approval list exists. Any wallet holding the required constituents can Strike
shares. Any wallet holding shares can Melt them without asking Seametry to
approve the participant.

<ClaimLine lead="The Hall reads quantities.">Strike and Melt do not use an oracle, a quote or NAV as an instruction input.</ClaimLine>
<ClaimLine lead="Delivery stays explicit.">A constituent held back by its issuer remains a named Claim and can be withdrawn when delivery resumes.</ClaimLine>
